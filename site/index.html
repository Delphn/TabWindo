Privacy

Private by design.

A window switcher can see everything on your screen. TabWindo is built to look as little as possible, and to show you exactly what it does.

Some window switchers capture your screen all day to keep previews ready. TabWindo takes pictures only while you’re switching.

Pictures only while you switch

Previews are taken while the panel is on screen, never on a quick tap. They stay in memory and are never saved.

Nothing leaves your Mac

There’s no network code at all: no analytics, no accounts, no updater.

Two permissions, both explained

Accessibility, to see your windows and bring one forward. Screen Recording, only for previews: skip it and you get app icons instead.

Your ⌘Tab is always safe

If TabWindo quits, crashes or stops responding, macOS gets its own ⌘Tab back right away.

Checked by Apple

Signed with a Developer ID and notarized, so macOS verifies it before it ever opens.

Questions

Why does TabWindo need Accessibility access?

To see your windows and bring the one you pick to the front. macOS asks once, and you can turn it off in System Settings at any time.

Do I have to allow Screen Recording?

Only if you want live previews. Without it, TabWindo works exactly the same and shows app icons instead.

What happens to the built-in ⌘Tab?

With ⌘Tab chosen, TabWindo takes over the shortcut while it runs. Quit it, or if it ever crashes or stops responding, and macOS’s own ⌘Tab comes right back. ⌥Tab and ⌃Tab leave it alone.

Why isn’t it on the Mac App Store?

App Store apps run in a sandbox that blocks the Accessibility access needed to bring a specific window forward. So TabWindo is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple instead, like many Mac utilities.

Is it really free?

Yes. TabWindo is open source under the MIT License: free to use, read and change.
